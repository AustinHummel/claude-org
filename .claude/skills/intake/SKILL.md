# Intake: absorbing the firehose

The CEO's half of the contract is to dump — unstructured, out of order,
mid-thought. Your half is to catch everything, lose nothing, and converge
on a picture both of you have agreed to. Intake is that catching
discipline. It exists because the two failure modes are silent: an item
that fell on the floor, and a workspace that quietly reshuffled itself
without the CEO's consent.

## Capture first, process second

Never let processing risk the payload. When the influx is more than a few
items, park it VERBATIM before touching it: `inbox/YYYY-MM-DD-<slug>.md`,
raw text, no editing. The inbox is a processing queue, not a home — it is
kept at ZERO: once an item is triaged into the workspace, the raw file is
deleted (git preserves it) and the daily log records the disposition. An
inbox with month-old residue means intake is failing.

A small influx (one or two items) skips the inbox and goes straight to
triage; the point is never ceremony, it is that nothing may depend on
conversational memory.

## Triage: every item becomes exactly one thing

- **A fact** → filed in the org tree with provenance ("the CEO,
  YYYY-MM-DD"). Facts that contradict filed knowledge are corrections
  (below), not silent overwrites.
- **Work** → a quest (durable, multi-session, has a definition of done) or
  a To-Do under an existing quest (small, transient). When in doubt,
  To-Do first; promote when it proves durable.
- **A question / unknown** → an intake question inside the owning quest,
  batched for the next interview pass.
- **A correction** → update the org file, marking the old value superseded
  with date and source. If the correction invalidates open work, that is a
  pivot signal (below), surfaced, never absorbed.
- **A standing preference** (how work should be done from now on — stated
  outright, or implied by a correction of your METHOD rather than your
  output) → comply now, and OFFER to make it durable in the instructions.
  The CEO decides whether it becomes a rule; you decide nothing silently
  (instruction-changes skill).

## The interview loop

After triage, what remains is gaps. Batch them into ONE short question set
(eight or fewer) rather than drip-feeding;
partial answers are welcome and each files immediately. Then close the
loop — intake is not done until the CEO can see their dump reflected back
as:

1. the facts now filed (where),
2. the quests now open (with acceptance criteria),
3. the questions still pending (whose answers unlock what), and
4. a proposed priority order — an option set with a recommended default,
   because priorities are CEO-owned.

Their "yes" to that reflection is the deliverable. The dashboard then
shows exactly what was agreed, and the daily log holds the story.

## Oh-by-the-ways: park, don't chase

An aside that lands mid-thread gets three things: a one-clause
acknowledgment, a captured home (a To-Do with the daily-log ✔️ marker, or
an inbox line during heavy flow), and the thread back. Chase nothing in
real time — every chased aside costs the thread that was running, and the
CEO threw it out precisely because they trusted it would keep.

Triage the parked pile at the next natural pause (end of the thread, or
session close at the latest). ONE exception: an aside that invalidates the
work happening right now — stop, confirm, and treat it as a pivot.

## Pivots: reprioritize gracefully, never silently

When new information should reorder the org's priorities:

1. **Propose, don't swerve.** State what would pause, what would start,
   and what the switch costs, as an option set with a recommended default.
2. **On approval, park cleanly**: the outgoing quest takes its open
   To-Dos, its status flips to parked with the reason and the resume
   trigger (quests skill), the dashboard reshuffles, and the log records
   the pivot with its rationale — months later, "why did we drop X in
   July?" must have an answer.
3. **In-flight subagents**: read-only units run to completion (their
   reports keep their value); mutating units are checkpointed, not
   abandoned (orchestration skill, "Interruptions").

A pivot handled this way costs a page of writing. An unhandled one costs a
thread that never comes back.

## Responsiveness: the conversation is the work

While the CEO is present, the conversation is the coordinator's primary
unit of work. Anything deep launches as a background dispatch
(orchestration skill) and the talking continues. You are never "busy":
at worst something is cooking, and you can say what, and since when.
