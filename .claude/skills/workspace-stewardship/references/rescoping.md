# Re-rooting: when the org outgrows its mission

The growth ladder promotes children as they grow. This spoke covers the
inverse: the ROOT is outgrown. An org instantiated for one project (an
integration, a repair, a single venture) starts receiving work its mission
doesn't contain, and the true org turns out to sit a level ABOVE the one
that was built. Re-rooting is how the org zooms out without losing any of
its depth — and why scoping an org small at genesis is always safe.

## The signals

- The filing rules can only place incoming work "least-wrong", repeatedly.
- The mission in `CLAUDE.md` now describes a subset of the open quests and
  live areas.
- Two live areas relate to each other only through the principal, not
  through the mission (a thermostat project and a tax question share
  nothing but "mine").
- The principal says it outright ("help me with home projects generally").

One stray directive is not a signal; file it least-wrong and watch. A
pattern of them is.

## The decision (principal-owned)

Re-rooting changes what the org IS, so it is proposed, never assumed:
present the options with a recommended default, per the status-report
answer shape. The options:

- **Re-root in place.** This repo's mission broadens; the current mission
  descends to become one child. Right while the org is young and light.
- **Supersede.** A new, broader org is instantiated (from the template),
  and this org becomes a registered child of it, untouched. Right once
  this org is its own repo with real history — the federated topology
  makes this the cheap, surgery-free path.
- **Seed a sibling.** The new scope is not a parent but a PEER with its own
  lifecycle or stakes (a legal entity, a business, a different hat the
  principal wears). It gets its own org, seeded by CITING this org's
  knowledge.
- **Decline.** Keep the scope; keep filing; revisit when the pattern is
  clearer.

## Mechanics: re-root in place

1. Descend the old mission: create one child area (or sub-org, if it keeps
   its own cadence) named for it, and move the mission-specific areas and
   spokes beneath it. Quests stay in the root `quests/` unless the child
   is promoted to a full sub-org with volume of its own.
2. Rewrite the root contract: the mission broadens to the discovered
   scope; standing facts update; the old mission survives as a one-line
   pointer to its child.
3. Regroup the dashboard: lanes become per-child digests where the count
   demands it.
4. Maps updated and links walked in the same round (doc-hygiene's
   reorganizing rules); the whole re-root is one isolated, committed unit.
5. The chronicle never moves: the daily logs stay root-continuous — the
   org didn't die, it grew — and the re-root itself is logged with its
   signals, the principal's directive as origination, and the new shape.

## Mechanics: supersede

1. Instantiate the broader org fresh from the template; its mission is the
   discovered scope.
2. Register this org as a child: the standard pointer file in the owning
   area (path/URL, one-line purpose, where its contract lives).
3. Narrow this org's contract to what it truly owns and add the parent
   pointer (what it reports up). Its files, logs, and git history stay
   byte-intact — the org becomes a frozen-when-idle seat of the new root.
4. Move UP only the knowledge that belongs to the broader scope; leave
   domain knowledge where it lives. When something must exist in both,
   the child stays authoritative and the parent carries a citation, never
   a copy that can drift.
5. The parent seeds its dashboard lane from this org's dashboard, and its
   genesis log records the supersession on both sides (each org logs it
   from its own altitude).

## Seeding a sibling

A sibling org bootstraps normally, then seeds its knowledge by CITING the
source org with provenance ("per <org>'s <file>, as of <date>"), never by
wholesale copying. The source org stays authoritative for its own domain;
the sibling accumulates only what is genuinely its own. Record the kinship
in both orgs' maps (a pointer each way) so a future coordinator on either
side knows the other exists.

## The invariants

- **A re-root narrows no one.** Every child keeps its scope, its contract,
  and its memory; expertise is preserved exactly where it accrued. The
  root only gains altitude — and its per-domain knowledge should get
  THINNER as its span widens, because depth belongs to the children.
- **The chronicle is continuous.** Logs never migrate between repos;
  structure moves, history doesn't. Git plus the log index keep every
  pre-re-root day reachable.
- **The root stays provisional.** Nothing prevents a second or third
  re-root as the principal's scope keeps growing; the protocol is the
  same at any altitude. Never pre-build the wider org on speculation —
  structure materializes under real pressure, and this protocol is what
  makes that discipline safe.
